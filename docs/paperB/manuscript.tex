\title{Effective line redistribution in kilonova ejecta:\\
       transport-relevant structure is frequency-local}

\begin{abstract}
Lanthanide line fluorescence in kilonova ejecta couples of order $10^6$
transitions, and radiative-transfer calculations therefore replace it with a
closure. On identical resolved Sobolev opacity, with an energy-conserving
downward macroatom as the reference, we show that no single thermalisation
parameter $\epsilon$ reproduces the emergent photometry, while a
group-to-group redistribution operator with a handful of contiguous frequency
groups does. Comparing local frequency coarsening with a global non-negative
factorisation at a matched number of archetypal exit distributions, the local
representation reaches the \PBdmMax~mag criterion with no more archetypes
than the global one, and on Nd\,\textsc{ii} with far fewer; at
\PBContrastK\ archetypes the global operator reproduces the microscopic
redistribution better while reproducing the light worse. The structure that
matters for transport is frequency-local. It is not, however, universal: an
operator built at one thermodynamic state does not remain predictive when the
gas temperature, density or the physical trajectory changes, whereas an
operator rebuilt at the new state does, and interpolating the whole operator
between two neighbouring states recovers most of the difference --- but not
along a coupled trajectory through state space, and not at a fixed resolution
for Ce\,\textsc{ii}. Per-species operators do not compose into a mixture
without refitting, yet the full \PBBlendNIons-ion mixture itself is described
by \PBBlendKrec\ groups where the best scalar closure misses by
\PBBlendEpsErr~mag. Detailed lanthanide fluorescence thus has a much lower
observable complexity than its microscopic transition network: the reduced
description is frequency-local and remains compact across physical states and
realistic mixtures, but its parameters, and sometimes its required
resolution, depend on the thermodynamic and radiation-field state.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The optical and infrared light of a kilonova is shaped by the line opacity
of the lanthanides synthesised in the ejecta, whose open $f$-shells carry
of order $10^6$ transitions per ion at the relevant temperatures
\citep{barneskasen2013,tanaka2020}. A photon absorbed in one of those lines
is re-emitted, after a cascade through the ion's levels, in another; this
fluorescence redistributes energy across the spectrum and is the dominant
non-local process in the transport. No calculation follows every
transition. Every kilonova radiative-transfer code replaces the fluorescence
by a closure: a single thermalisation parameter $\epsilon$ that sends a
fraction of the absorbed energy to a thermal pool and scatters the rest
coherently \citep{fontes2020}, an expansion-opacity treatment that groups
the lines and prescribes the re-emission within a group, or a macroatom that
walks the cascade explicitly \citep{lucy2002,lucy2003}. In a companion
methods paper we showed, on published benchmark ejecta states and with an
energy-conserving treatment of the fluorescence, that the coarse
line-opacity closures leave errors of order a magnitude in the broad-band
photometry and that the errors change shape when the fluorescence
prescription changes (Paper IV). % TODO: cite once submitted
The question that leaves open is whether the fluorescence itself --- not
the opacity --- admits a compact description at all, and if so what it
depends on.

We ask that question in the narrowest form we can control. The opacity is
held fixed at the resolved Sobolev value of every line; the reference is an
energy-conserving downward macroatom; and the candidate closure is a
redistribution operator $R_{ij}$ that maps the frequency group of an
absorbed packet to the frequency group and exit line of the re-emitted one.
The operator is built from the reference's own Monte Carlo events on one
set of random seeds and scored on another, so that no operator is ever
compared with the events it was trained on. Three questions follow one
another. Does such an operator exist, and is it better than the best scalar
closure? Why does it work --- is the structure it exploits locality in
frequency, or a globally low rank of the microscopic redistribution law?
And how far does it generalise: across thermodynamic state, across the
radiation field, and across composition?

Each question was posed as a preregistered gate with its thresholds, its
gray conditions and its readings fixed before the run, and the record of
every run, every reading and every amendment made during execution is
public (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The answers are, in order: a
frequency-local operator with a handful of groups reproduces the reference
where no scalar closure comes within a magnitude
(Section~\ref{sec:compression}); the compression is frequency locality, and
a global representation that reproduces the microscopic events better
reproduces the emergent light worse (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}); and the
operator is compact everywhere but universal nowhere --- its parameters, and
for one ion its required resolution, depend on the state, in a way that a
table over a few coordinates captures along single coordinates but not
along a coupled trajectory, while the realistic mixture remains as compact
as any single ion (Section~\ref{sec:generality}). The result is a hierarchy:
millions of transitions, to a fine distribution of redistribution events, to
a small state-dependent local operator, to the observables --- with most of
the information discarded between the middle two steps and no harm done to
the last.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{State and atom.} Every calculation is a single-zone,
time-independent transport through one shell of a published kilonova ejecta
model at two days --- the photospheric shell of the lanthanide-rich secular
component used as benchmark P1 in Paper IV --- with the shell's own density,
gas temperature and expansion time, and a black-body core at the gas
temperature illuminating it. The atom is one ion at its number density in
that shell, its levels and lines from the GSI calibrated line list
\citep{gsi_atomic}, level populations in local thermodynamic equilibrium,
and every line with Sobolev optical depth above $10^{-3}$ carrying opacity.
La\,\textsc{ii}, Ce\,\textsc{ii} and Nd\,\textsc{ii} are the three ions; the
first is thin enough at its partial density that every closure agrees, and
is carried as a control. The generality test of
Section~\ref{sec:generality} moves one coordinate of this state at a time
and, for the mixtures, replaces the single ion by the three ions together
and by the state's full \PBBlendNIons-ion composition.

\paragraph{Transport and reference.} Packets carry indivisible energy and
are transported with worldline relativity and Sobolev line interaction
(Paper IV). The reference fluorescence is a downward macroatom: on
absorption the packet activates the line's upper level and walks the
radiative cascade with branching probabilities $A\beta$, the escape
probability $\beta$ of each line included, until it exits in a line whose
photon escapes; energy is conserved at every step by construction, and the
identity between injected and accounted energy is verified to
$10^{-10}$ on every run. A radiation-field-driven macroatom with internal
upward transitions under an imposed diluted Planck field was run once as a
robustness check (Section~\ref{sec:compression}), and a scalar closure
$\epsilon$ --- thermal re-emission from the energy-weighted line emissivity
with probability $\epsilon$, coherent scattering otherwise --- was scanned
over a grid of values to find the best one for each ion.

\paragraph{The operator.} The redistribution operator is built from the
reference's events, each event the absorbed comoving frequency, the
re-emitted rest frequency and the packet's energy before and after. On
$N_g$ log-spaced frequency groups the energy transition matrix $R_{ij}$ is
the fraction of energy absorbed in group $i$ that exits in group $j$; within
each output group the exit line is drawn from a discrete table of the
distinct exit lines the events populated, weighted by their energy. Transport
with the operator replaces the macroatom walk by one draw from $R_{ij}$ and
one from the table. Operators are built from events on three build seeds and
scored on three disjoint evaluation seeds. The global family of
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} replaces the transition matrix by a rank-$k$
non-negative factorisation of the fine-grouped matrix, iterated to
convergence, on the same exit tables; the interpolated operators of
Section~\ref{sec:generality} combine two endpoint operators linearly in the
logarithm of the moved coordinate, transition rows, deposition channel and
exit tables alike, on the union of the two endpoints' exit lines.

\paragraph{Metrics and gates.} Every closure is scored against the reference
on the same state and seeds by the maximum and mean absolute error in the
broad-band magnitudes over the bands that carry at least one per cent of the
luminosity with acceptable Monte Carlo precision, by the maximum absolute
error in the colours between those bands, by an integrated spectral
distance, and by an energy-weighted total-variation distance between the
operator and an independent fine-grouped matrix from the evaluation events.
The criterion used throughout is a maximum band error of \PBdmMax~mag and a
maximum colour error of \PBdcolMax~mag. Each of the three questions was
preregistered as a gate --- the state, the grids, the metrics, the
thresholds, the gray conditions that invalidate a reading, and the readings
themselves fixed and committed before the run --- and read gray-first. Three
amendments were made during the runs: two implementation defects in the
factorisation were found on the control ion and fixed before any decisive
number was read; a validity control fired and its preregistered remedy was
applied; and a sampler overflow found on the mixture run was fixed, its
reachability in every earlier record established by inspection, and the
affected calculation repeated. Each is recorded, with what had been seen
when it was made, in the archived preregistration.

\section{A scalar closure is insufficient; a small operator is not}
\label{sec:compression}
% Step 1 and step 2, from G1 (F67) and the robustness check (F68).
The optimally tuned scalar misses the reference by \PBCeEpsErr~mag on
Ce\,\textsc{ii} and \PBNdEpsErr~mag on Nd\,\textsc{ii}, while the group
operator meets the \PBdmMax~mag criterion at $N_g=\PBCeNgStar$ and
$N_g=\PBNdNgStar$ respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:compression}a,
Table~\ref{tab:gates}). La\,\textsc{ii} at its partial density in this state
interacts \PBLaEvPerPkt\ times per packet and does not discriminate the
closures; it is carried as a control throughout.

The result is not a property of one cascade prescription. Replacing the
reference by a radiation-field-driven macroatom with internal upward
transitions moves the emergent photometry by \PBCeShift~mag on
Ce\,\textsc{ii} and \PBNdShift~mag on Nd\,\textsc{ii}; an operator rebuilt
from that reference's own events follows it to \PBCeRebuilt\ and
\PBNdRebuilt~mag, while the operator trained on the original reference stays
\PBCeDownward~mag away, reproducing the physics it was built from
(Fig.~\ref{fig:compression}b).

\section{Why it works: locality, not low rank}
\label{sec:mechanism}
% Step 3, from G2 (F69). The conceptual centre of the paper.
Two families of operator were built on identical exit tables and identical
opacity, differing only in the group-to-group matrix: local coarsening into
$N_g$ contiguous frequency blocks, and a global non-negative factorisation
into $k$ freely mixed archetypes. At a matched count the global family is far
the more expressive, carrying \PBParamsGlobal\ free numbers against
\PBParamsLocal\ at \PBContrastK\ archetypes. It nonetheless needs no fewer
archetypes to reach the criterion: $K^\star_{\rm local}=\PBCeKlocal$ against
$K^\star_{\rm global}=\PBCeKglobal$ on Ce\,\textsc{ii}, and
$K^\star_{\rm local}=\PBNdKlocal$ on Nd\,\textsc{ii} against a global family
that is \PBNdKglobal\ at every rank tried.

The contrast runs the other way on the microscopic distribution
(Table~\ref{tab:contrast}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}). At \PBContrastK\
archetypes the global operator's event-level total-variation loss is
\PBNdEventGlobal\ against the local operator's \PBNdEventLocal\ on
Nd\,\textsc{ii} --- it reproduces the microscopic redistribution
\PBNdEventRatio\ times better --- while its photometric error is
\PBNdBandGlobal~mag against \PBNdBandLocal~mag, \PBNdBandRatio\ times worse.
The degrees of freedom that minimise a generic distance between microscopic
redistribution distributions are therefore not the degrees of freedom that
carry the radiation field.

% The PI's wording, kept deliberately: the claim is NOT that millions of
% transitions collapse to a few numbers.
The compressibility is two-level. Writing
$P(\nu_{\rm out}\mid\nu_{\rm in}) = P(j\mid i)\,P(\nu_{\rm out}\mid i,j)$,
the first factor is extremely coarse, while the second still needs a sparse
but appreciable set of real exit lines: truncating the exit tables by
retained energy, the emergent light needs \PBCeLstar\ of the distinct exit
lines on Ce\,\textsc{ii} and \PBNdLstar\ on Nd\,\textsc{ii} at the same
thresholds. The transport-relevant organisation of fluorescence is
low-dimensional and frequency-local, even though the microscopic exit
spectrum is not equally compressible.

\section{Generality: a compact operator that depends on the state}
\label{sec:generality}
% Step 4, from G3 (F70). The PI's progression: state dependence ->
% interpolation -> the coupled trajectory -> composition.
The first three sections concern one thermodynamic state. The last question
is whether the operator built there is a property of the ion or of the state
it was built in. We moved one coordinate at a time away from that state along
\PBNAxes\ axes --- the gas temperature that sets the level populations, the
number density that sets the optical-depth scale, the temperature of the
illuminating source that sets which input groups the light actually uses,
and the physical trajectory of the ejecta at one, three and five days, along
which every coordinate moves together --- \PBNStatesPerIon\ states per ion in
all, on one frequency support per ion frozen over the whole domain. At every
state two operators were scored against that state's own reference
(Fig.~\ref{fig:generality}, Table~\ref{tab:generality}): the operator built
at the original state and transported unchanged, which tests
\emph{transfer}, and an operator rebuilt from the new state's own events,
which tests whether the compact description still \emph{exists} there. The
two tests were kept apart by design, because their failures mean different
things. Both use \PBNgT\ groups for every ion, one controlled representation
size across the comparison.

\paragraph{Transfer fails; compression does not.} The transported operator
is not predictive. For Ce\,\textsc{ii} it fails the criterion on every axis;
for Nd\,\textsc{ii} and the La\,\textsc{ii} control it survives only along the
source-spectrum axis, the one coordinate that changes which rows of the
operator are illuminated without changing the atom's response. Anywhere the
populations or the optical depths move, an operator trained elsewhere is
simply the wrong operator: \PBNdFixT~mag on Nd\,\textsc{ii} at the interior
temperature point, midway in $\log T_{\rm gas}$ between two states at which
the operator was rebuilt, and larger at the ends of every axis. That kills the strongest reading of
Sections~\ref{sec:compression}--\ref{sec:mechanism}: there is no universal
$R_{ij}$. But the operator rebuilt at the new state passes at every one of
the \PBNdNStates\ readable Nd\,\textsc{ii} states and every one of the
\PBLaNStates\ La\,\textsc{ii} states, with at most \PBNdKrecMax\ groups
needed anywhere on Nd\,\textsc{ii}. The failure of transfer is not a failure
of compression. What varies with the state is the content of a small
operator, not its size.

\paragraph{Interpolation: $R = R(\theta)$.} If the operator's parameters vary
smoothly with the state, a table over a few coordinates should replace it.
At the interior grid point of each axis we built an operator from the two
bracketing states alone --- never from the interior state's own events ---
by interpolating the whole effective operator linearly in the logarithm of
the moved coordinate: the transition rows, the deposition channel, and the
conditional exit-frequency weights on the union of the two endpoints' exit
lines. That interpolant passes the criterion at every interior point on the
temperature, density and source-spectrum axes for every ion, reducing
Nd\,\textsc{ii}'s transfer error at the interior temperature from
\PBNdFixT\ to \PBNdIntWT~mag and Ce\,\textsc{ii}'s at the interior density
from \PBCeFixD\ to \PBCeIntWD~mag. The physically useful object is therefore
a state-dependent family $R(\theta)$ over a small set of coordinates, and
along a single coordinate that family is smooth enough to tabulate. The
conditional exit spectrum is part of what must be tabulated: interpolating
the transition matrix alone while keeping one endpoint's exit tables leaves
Ce\,\textsc{ii} at \PBCeIntMD~mag on the density axis, above the criterion,
where the whole-operator interpolant passes at \PBCeIntWD.

\paragraph{The coupled trajectory is the boundary of that statement.} The
interpolation test above moves one coordinate at a time. Along the physical
trajectory, where temperature, density and the source all change together,
Nd\,\textsc{ii} at three days is the one point in the domain where
interpolation fails: the transported operator misses by \PBNdTrajAnchor~mag,
the whole-operator interpolant by \PBNdTrajWhole~mag, and the matrix-only
interpolant \PBNdTrajMatrixPasses\ as well, on colour rather than on band ---
while the operator rebuilt at that state passes at \PBNdTrajKrec\ groups with
\PBNdTrajFresh~mag. This is not a failure of compactness; it is a failure of
separable interpolation. The operator manifold cannot necessarily be
reconstructed by treating the state coordinates independently, and we do not
claim that interpolation works in general: it was tested primarily one axis
at a time, and the one coupled path tested breaks it.

\paragraph{The required resolution is itself state-dependent.}
Ce\,\textsc{ii} adds a separate qualification. Its rebuilt \PBNgT-group
operator fails the criterion at \PBCeNFreshFail\ of its \PBCeNStates\ states
--- at every one of them on colour, by between \PBCeLeastColourOver\ and
\PBCeWorstColourOver~mag over the threshold, and at \PBCeNFreshFailBand\ of
them on band as well --- and at every one of them a \PBCeKrecMax-group
operator passes. Section~\ref{sec:compression} found Ce\,\textsc{ii}'s
$N_g^\star=\PBCeNgStar$ exactly at the edge of the criterion at the original
state; across the domain that marginality becomes a resolution requirement
that moves with the state, $N_g^\star(\theta)$. Compactness survives, but a
fixed representation size does not, and a practical table for
Ce\,\textsc{ii} needs either adaptive resolution or the conservative
\PBCeKrecMax-group representation throughout. La\,\textsc{ii}, whose
interactions per packet are too few to discriminate any closure, passes
everywhere and decides nothing.

\paragraph{Composition.} Two mixture tests close the gate. First, at the
original state the three single-ion operators were combined by the
opacity-share composition rule of the earlier photon-packet work, with no fit
to the blend, and scored against the three-ion blend's own macroatom: the
composed operator does not reach the criterion at any valid tested
resolution (\PBAmixBestBand~mag at its best, \PBAmixBestN\ groups; the
finest-resolution run was excluded by the preregistered validity check),
while an operator
trained on the blend's own events passes at \PBKdirect\ groups
(\PBAdirectBand~mag). Per-species operators are not trivially composable.
Second, and decisively, on the full \PBBlendNIons-ion composition of the
published benchmark state the operator built from the blend's events passes
at \PBBlendKrec\ groups (\PBBlendBand~mag on band, \PBBlendColour~mag on
colour), while the best scalar closure --- again the coherent limit,
$\epsilon^\star=\PBBlendEps$ --- misses by \PBBlendEpsErr~mag, the widest
scalar failure in this work. Read together, the two results are not in
tension: the combined physical system is itself highly compressible even
though it cannot be assembled from its parts without refitting. It is this
result that keeps the state dependence above from reducing the paper to a
negative finding about transfer. The physically relevant mixture still
admits a small effective description.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:discussion}
The three gates together establish a hierarchy. Of the order $10^6$
transitions of a lanthanide ion, the transport sees a fine distribution of
redistribution events; of that distribution, the emergent light sees a
small operator; and that operator is local in frequency. Most of the
information in the event distribution is discarded on the way to the light
without harm --- the global factorisation of Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}
reproduces the events \PBNdEventRatio\ times better than the local operator
and the photometry \PBNdBandRatio\ times worse --- so fidelity to the
microscopic redistribution is the wrong target for a closure. The right
target is the frequency-local structure, and a handful of contiguous groups
carries it.

What Section~\ref{sec:generality} adds is that the small operator is not one
object but a family. Its parameters depend on the gas temperature, the
density and the illumination, and the dependence is smooth enough along any
one of those coordinates that an operator interpolated between two
neighbouring states passes the same criterion as one built in place. That
is the practically useful form of the result: not a universal $R_{ij}$, but
a tabulated $R(\theta)$ over a few state coordinates, of the same size at
every entry. Two boundaries of that statement are set by the data and should
be read as results rather than caveats. Along the physical trajectory, where
the coordinates move together, one-dimensional interpolation fails at the
one interior point tested while a fresh fit succeeds --- the family is
compact but not separable, and a table indexed by independent coordinates is
not guaranteed to reconstruct it. And for Ce\,\textsc{ii} the resolution the
criterion requires is itself state-dependent: \PBNgT\ groups suffice at the
original state and fail at half of the others, \PBCeKrecMax\ groups pass
everywhere. A closure of this kind must carry its resolution as a parameter
or adopt the conservative one.

The mixture results settle how the family composes. It does not: operators
built for single ions, combined by the opacity-share rule that served the
earlier photon-packet work, do not reproduce the blend they are combined
into, while an operator built on the blend does, at \PBKdirect\ groups. Yet
the full \PBBlendNIons-ion composition of the benchmark state --- the
mixture a transport calculation actually needs --- is described by
\PBBlendKrec\ groups where the best scalar closure misses by
\PBBlendEpsErr~mag. Per-species operators are not composable, but the
combined system is compressible, and the effective description a code would
use is the one built on the mixture it transports.

Three limitations bound the claims. The calculations are single-zone and
time-independent, with the level populations in local thermodynamic
equilibrium and the radiation field imposed rather than solved; the
operator's dependence on a self-consistent radiation field is measured only
through the source-spectrum axis. The state domain is a grid of single
coordinates around one benchmark state and one coupled path; the failure of
interpolation along that path is one data point, and it does not yet
indicate what representation would succeed there --- a better choice of
coordinates or a denser table are the first candidates, ahead of any
learned surrogate. And the criterion is photometric: an operator that passes
it reproduces the broad-band light, not the line-by-line spectrum.

The conclusion we draw is nevertheless a positive one for transport.
Detailed lanthanide fluorescence has a much lower observable complexity
than its microscopic transition network. The reduced description is
frequency-local, survives a change of the fluorescence physics it is built
from, and remains compact across physical states and realistic mixtures.
What it does not do is transfer: its parameters, and sometimes its required
resolution, depend on the thermodynamic and radiation-field state, and a
transport code that adopts it must carry that dependence --- as a table
along the coordinates where the dependence is smooth, and as a refit where
it is not.
